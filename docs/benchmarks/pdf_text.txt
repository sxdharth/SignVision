1Abstract
The proposed project,SignVision: An Intelligent Multimodal Communication and Au-
tomation System, aims to develop an intelligent computer vision-based platform capable of en-
abling accessible and inclusive communication and environmental control for the deaf and hard-of-
hearing community. Such environments include remote teleconferencing, digital workspaces, and
personal living spaces, where seamless interaction requires multimodal accessibility. Unlike con-
ventional video conferencing tools that rely primarily on auditory and spoken communication, the
system incorporates spatial-temporal deep learning, machine translation, and WebRTC to support
real-time sign language translation into multiple spoken languages, including English, Spanish,
Hindi, and Malayalam.
In its current iteration, the system follows a pipeline-based approach in which raw video input is
processed sequentially through an optimized AI inference engine. The process begins with video
frame capture via a WebRTC signaling server, followed by precise spatial feature extraction using
Google MediaPipe to map hand, facial, and pose landmarks. Temporal sequence buffering is
managed using a dynamic sliding-window mechanism, and gesture-to-text classification is performed
using either a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) or Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (Bi-LSTM)
neural network. The extracted text is then processed by a natural language translation module
to convert the base English predictions into the user’s preferred target language. The translated
text is subsequently converted into audible speech using a robust Text-to-Speech (TTS) engine,
ensuring natural auditory output for non-signing participants.
Additionally, a bidirectional Text-to-Sign feature retrieves corresponding sign language video demon-
strations to support visual interaction. To enhance physical accessibility, an integrated IoT bridge
maps specific recognized gestures to serial commands, which trigger an Arduino microcontroller to
actuate smart home hardware relays. The proposed architecture offers several advantages, includ-
ing robust real-time inference, hardware-agnostic browser deployment, and efficient multimodal
processing, making it highly suitable for real-world deployment in both telecommunication and
automated home environments.Contents

Introduction
Communication is a fundamental human right, yet traditional spoken interactions remain a pro-
found barrier for the Deaf, Hard of Hearing, and Speech-Impaired communities. While modern
technology has connected the globe, individuals with sensory or linguistic disabilities often face
social and professional isolation due to the general population’s lack of sign language proficiency.
Current assistive tools usually act as static dictionaries or isolated, one-way translators, fundamen-
tally lacking the interactive fluidity necessary for natural, real-time human connection. SignVision
is developed to eliminate these systemic communication barriers by introducing a comprehensive,
AI-powered accessibility platform. It achieves this by directly integrating real-time, bi-directional
American Sign Language (ASL) translation into a robust WebRTC video-calling framework. Uti-
lizing advanced Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) neural networks alongside MediaPipe holistic
tracking, SignVision instantly translates complex sign sequences into text and synthesized audio,
while simultaneously converting spoken words into readable captions to ensure a seamless two-way
digital communication experience.
1.1 Motivation
The primary motivation driving SignVision is the urgent need to establish agenuinely inclusive
digital ecosystemwhere sensory and physical differences no longer impede social integration or
professional opportunity. Despite rapid advancements in artificial intelligence, today’s mainstream
video conferencing platforms remain largely inaccessible to sign language users, forcing them to rely
on costly, privacy-intrusivethird-party human interpretersfor basic everyday communication.
Existing technological alternatives are severely fragmented—providing either isolatedsign-to-text
translationwithout a live communication feed, or video capabilities entirely devoid ofcogni-
tive assistive features. SignVision was conceived to bridge this critical gap through aunified,
privacy-first platformthat operates intuitively for signers and non-signers alike. By building
an accessible architecture that enablesreal-time bi-directional connectionand establishes the

1.2 Problem Statement
Most digital communication platforms do not support people with disabilities because they lack
real-time, two-way translation and easy-to-use adaptive interfaces. As a result, these users cannot
communicate independently or control their digital and physical environments without assistance,
creating a major barrier to equal access and inclusion.
TheSignVisionproject seeks to address the following critical challenges:
•Significant Communication Barriers:A profound communication gap exists between the
Deaf, Hard of Hearing, and Speech-Impaired communities and the general population due to
a pervasive lack of sign language proficiency.
•Inaccessible Mainstream Platforms:Current video conferencing platforms are inaccessi-
ble to sign language users without relying on costly and privacy-intrusive third-party human
interpreters.
•Fragmented Assistive Technologies:Existing AI translation tools typically function as
isolated, one-way solutions that fail to integrate seamlessly into live, two-way conversations.
•Lack of Environmental Control:Current assistive ecosystems rarely provide adaptive
profiles for compounding disabilities, and fail to offer integrated capabilities for navigating
and controlling physical surroundings.
To address these gaps, the proposed system aims to:
•Real-Time AI Translation Engine: A core neural network module that instantly processes
live video feeds to translate American Sign Language (ASL) and finger-spelling into readable
text.
•Web-Based Video Calling Platform: A custom, browser-accessible communication platform
utilizing WebRTC for secure, peer-to-peer video conferencing without the need for external
plugins.
•Enable Multi-Modal Accessibility:Incorporate integrated Speech-to-Text (STT) and
Text-to-Speech (TTS) to ensure instant captioning of spoken words and verbalization of
translated signs.

Literature Review
2.1 Real-Time Continuous Sign Language Recognition Using LSTM
(Wang et al., 2021)
This paper explores continuous sign language recognition by leveraging Long Short-Term Memory
(LSTM) networks to capture the spatio-temporal dependencies of hand movements. It utilizes
sequential video frames to interpret complex sign patterns and continuous sentences rather than
isolated static gestures.
Advantages:
•Effectively captures temporal dynamics and motion context of continuous sign language.
•Demonstrates high accuracy in sequence-to-sequence gesture translation for fluid conversa-
tions.
Disadvantages:
•Computationally intensive when processing raw frames, making lightweight edge-deployment
challenging.
•Requires large datasets of annotated video sequences for robust model training.
Relevance to Our Project:
This work establishes the foundation for using LSTM networks in our AI inference engine to process
real-time, sequential American Sign Language (ASL) data for continuous and accurate translation.

Proposed Project
3.1 Objectives
The primary objective of this project is to create a robust, AI-powered bi-directional communi-
cation platform tailored for the Deaf, Hard of Hearing, and Speech-Impaired communities. It
prioritizes seamless accessibility, real-time gesture translation, and physical independence in both
digital conferencing environments and the physical world. Specific objectives include:
•To develop a modular AI pipeline that processes live video inputs through stages such as spa-
tial landmark extraction, sequential tracking, deep learning inference, and translated output
generation.
•To implement highly accurate spatial tracking using the MediaPipe framework to extract
holistic hand, facial, and pose landmarks without requiring heavy external sensors.
•To achieve accurate, continuous translation of American Sign Language (ASL) and finger-
spelled inputs using advanced Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) neural networks.
•To integrate the AI inference engine entirely within a WebRTC-based video conferencing
environment, facilitating ultra-low latency, fluid live conversations.
•To establish a true two-way communication bridge by utilizing Speech-to-Text (STT) for
instant live captioning and Text-to-Speech (TTS) for the vocalization of recognized signs.
•To design custom adaptive disability profiles (e.g., Deaf, Blind, Speech-Impaired) that dy-
namically adjust the user interface based on the user’s specific access needs.
•To implement gesture-based Smart Home (IoT) automation capabilities, allowing users to
intuitively command physical appliances directly through the visual recognition engine.

•To ensure strong user privacy and network efficiency through a secure architecture that pro-
cesses heavy media abstractions locally while reducing raw video transmission.
•To build a scalable and extensible system architecture that enables the continuous introduc-
tion of updated gesture vocabularies, new linguistic datasets, and broader hardware logic.
3.2 Proposed Solution
The proposed system,SignVision, is an inclusive, AI-driven bi-directional communication plat-
form designed to provide real-time translation of American Sign Language (ASL) during live user
interactions. The system is built utilizing an optimized pipeline architecture where continuous
video data and audio are processed efficiently to ensure fluid conversational dynamics.
The system converts complex sign language and spoken dialog into textual and audible responses
by combining continuous spatial tracking, temporal deep learning, and advanced WebRTC inte-
gration. This design empowers users to communicate organically and operate their environments
without relying on third-party interpreters or external interfaces.
3.2.1 Adaptive User Interface and Profiles
The system primarily supports flexible interaction tailored to various compounding disabilities.
Users can configure specific accessibility profiles, allowing the software to cater specifically to Deaf,
Blind, or Speech-Impaired individuals.
The interface dynamically adjusts to these customized profiles, offering specialized visual con-

4.4 Algorithm
The SignVision system follows a parallel pipeline algorithm that processes both continuous video
frames and live audio within a WebRTC environment to achieve bi-directional communication. The
step-by-step procedure is as follows:
1. Start the system and initialize the WebRTC conferencing environment.
2. Load the user’s customized adaptive disability profile (e.g., Deaf, Blind, Speech-Impaired) to
configure the user interface and modalities accordingly.
3. Access the client device’s camera and microphone to capture live video frames and audio.
4. Establish peer-to-peer connections to transmit the raw WebRTC media streams to other call
participants.
5.Process Visual Input (ASL to Text/Speech):
(a) Feed raw camera frames into the MediaPipe holistic tracking model.
(b) Extract precise 3D spatial coordinates for hand, facial, and body pose landmarks.
(c) Check for predefined static configuration gestures. If an accepted gesture is detected,
trigger the corresponding Smart Home (IoT) automation command.
(d) If no static command is detected, normalize the spatial coordinates and buffer them
chronologically over a fixed sequence of frames.
(e) Pass the buffered sequence into the trained Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) neural
network.
(f) Perform temporal sequence translation to predict the corresponding continuous ASL
sentence or finger-spelled word.
6.Process Audio Input (Speech to Text):
(a) Capture live spoken words from non-signing participants.
(b) Process the audio through the Speech-to-Text (STT) transcription engine.
7. Synchronize the generated translation data and captions across the network via WebSockets
to ensure all call participants receive results simultaneously.
8. Output the multi-modal responses through the configured adaptive UI:
•On-screen Text:Display the LSTM-translated ASL as readable English text.
•Audio (TTS):Synthesize the translated ASL text into spoken words using the Text-
to-Speech engine.

•Live Captions (STT):Render the transcribed speech audio as closed captions overlaid
on the video feed.
9. Continuously loop the visual and audio processing phases for the duration of the call.
10. Terminate the system processes and release hardware resources when the session ends.
